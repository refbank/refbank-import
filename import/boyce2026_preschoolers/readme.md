# Readme

## Citation
Boyce, V., Sparks, R. Z., & Frank, M. C. (2026, February 10). Preschoolers can coordinate with each other to communicate about novel referents. Retrieved from osf.io/preprints/psyarxiv/t76a5_v1

## Abstract

Learning language requires learning not only the content of language, but also how
to use language to communicate. Iterated reference games provide a window into such
skills, requiring rich communication as participants converge on mutually 
understandable names for initially novel referents. Some early experiments are 
interpreted as evidence that 4-5-year-old children cannot converge on the mutually
understandable names needed to succeed in an iterated reference game. Here, we 
revisit young children's referential communicative abilities using a simpler, 
child-friendly paradigm. Across 82 pairs of children (N=158), we found that 
4-5-year-olds could successfully establish reference with each other. Children 
were 81% accurate, and they often used descriptions similar to their partner's. 
These findings suggest that children’s capacity to construct effective referring 
expressions in novel contexts emerges earlier than previously thought, consistent 
with the view that children show early pragmatic competence in supportive contexts.

## Study details
3 expts 

1. 20 pairs + 7 exclusions, 3 blocks of 4 target; we have 27 games
2. 31 pairs + 6 exclusions, 4 blocks of 4 targets; we have 37 games
3. 31 pairs + 17 exclusions, 4 block of 4 targets (different targets this time); we have 48 games

+ lots of pilots and exclusions

All expts had RAs running the experiments in the room with the kids and talking to the kids
Echoing of descriptions was common in expt 1 and rare in 2 and 3. 

* participants recruited from preschool classrooms
* group size: always pairs
* no partner switches
* switch describer/matcher roles every trial
* feedback: 2-way choice, so right wrong tells what the right answer is, 
both got happy/frowny face 
* backchannel: free talking
* children would sometimes gesture and (try) to show each other the ipad

## Processing/import
* we use the cleaned up transcripts to include only at least vaguely game relevant text from the kids (and no RA scaffold)
* we exclude the practice rounds
* we use our privileged information access to align kids ids for those kids who played multiple games (across or within studies)
* to revisit, but for the kids who played repeatedly, we take the demogs at first encounter;
* we're missing demographics for a couple kids (but I don't think its worth trying to track down)
* we have timing for selections, but not messages

Text, selections, images, and demographics are all included.
