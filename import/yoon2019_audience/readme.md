## Citation
Yoon, S. O., & Brown‐Schmidt, S. (2019). Audience design in multiparty conversation. Cognitive science, 43(8), e12774.

## Abstract

How do speakers design what they say in order to communicate effectively with groups of addressees who vary in their background knowledge of the topic at hand? Prior findings indicate that when a speaker addresses a pair of listeners with discrepant knowledge, that speakers Aim Low, designing their utterances for the least knowledgeable of the two addressees. Here, we test the hypothesis that speakers will depart from an Aim Low approach in order to efficiently communicate with larger groups of interacting partners. Further, we ask whether the cognitive demands of tracking multiple conversational partners’ perspectives places limitations on successful audience design. We find that speakers can successfully track information about what up to four of their partners do and do not know in conversation. When addressing groups of 3–4 addressees at once, speakers design language based on the combined knowledge of the group. These findings point to an audience design process that simultaneously represents the perspectives of multiple other individuals and combines these representations in order to design utterances that strike a balance between the different needs of the individuals within the group.

## Study details
(K=knowledgeable, did the sort phase; N=naive, didn't do sort phase)

* across studies, we code group size as the total in the group, although this is
usually not how many people are participating at a given time

* there is no mention of feedback in the paper, so we assume that feedback on correctness was not given



Expt 1: 
* 28 groups of 4 people, (D + M1, M2, M3) (we have 24-28 games/condition)
* Sort phase
  - D and M1
  - (16 images to sort) x 4 reps
  - interactive (full backchannel)
  - in 2K1N, D and M2 do same
  - no mention of feedback
* test phase (choose 1 of 4 targets) (16 old, 8 filler=new) we do not import filler trials
  - D plus some number of matchers
  - 4 targets (3 from sort and 1 new)
  - full backchannel: "test trials were interactive and Matchers were allowed to give feedback and ask questions as needed"
  - no mention of feedback 
* conditions: 1K, 1N, 2K1N, or 1K2N
* each group did all conditions, role was maintained across conditions, image sets were changed between conditions
* (in our coding, we treat each group x image-set combo as a new game)

## Processing/import
We have transcripts, but we do not have which images were the targets when, what the images are, or what the matchers selected. 
We also don't have anything that matchers may have said. 

For cued test phase trials, we aren't always sure which naive matchers participate, so we assume it's the lowest number (to at least get the participant counts accurate). 

Each group of people played the game multiple times with different role configurations and targets. 

There's also some uncertainty on whether the order we have is the actual trial order or not.
